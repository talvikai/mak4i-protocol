# MAK-0004: Conflict Resolution Specification

---

## Abstract

This standard defines what MAK4I considers a conflict between two or
more applicable artifacts, and how a MAK4I-compatible system evaluates,
resolves, or escalates that conflict. It does not define how artifacts
are ranked for relevance, how context is selected for injection, or how
versions and lifecycle states are represented — those are the domain of
MAK-0002, MAK-0003, and MAK-0005 respectively.

---

## Motivation

An AI session may have more than one applicable artifact whose content
disagrees, in whole or in part. Without a defined resolution behavior,
different MAK4I implementations could resolve the same conflict
differently, silently pick a side without recording why, or repeatedly
re-surface a question a team has already answered. This standard exists
to make conflict handling predictable, auditable, and consistent across
implementations — not to make MAK4I "smarter" about which side is
correct.

---

## Scope

This standard covers:
- Defining what does and does not constitute a conflict
- How authority, scope, and lifecycle status participate in resolution
- When a system MAY resolve a conflict automatically
- When a system MUST escalate rather than guess
- What must be recorded when a conflict is resolved

---

## Non-Goals

MAK-0004 does NOT define:
- General relevance ranking (see MAK-0003 or a future standard)
- Context injection format or selection (see MAK-0002)
- Complete versioning mechanics (see MAK-0005 — MAK-0004 consumes
  lifecycle/version information rather than redefining it)
- Registry implementation
- Database implementation
- Model-specific behavior
- MCP transport behavior

---

## Terminology

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Conflict** | A state in which two or more applicable artifacts contain contradictory, incompatible, or mutually exclusive information — see §1 for the full definition, still TBD in detail |
| **Applicable artifact** | An artifact that a resolution process has determined is in scope for the current decision (scope determination itself is TBD — see §3) |
| **Authority** | The weight or precedence assigned to an artifact's source when two applicable artifacts disagree — see §2 |
| **Escalation** | Surfacing an unresolved conflict to a human or requesting party rather than resolving it automatically — see §8 |
| **Resolution record** | The stored outcome of a conflict resolution event — see §10 |

*Additional terms inherited from [MAK-0001](MAK-0001.md): artifact,
`id`, `version`, `type`, `deprecated`, `deprecated_by`.*

---

## Design Principles

- Recency alone MUST NOT be the universal resolution mechanism (§5).
- A more specific scope MAY override a broader default, but MUST NOT
  automatically override a broader authoritative constraint such as an
  organization-wide security requirement (§3).
- When authority, scope, and lifecycle status do not clearly resolve a
  conflict, the system MUST escalate rather than guess (§8).
- Previously settled decisions SHOULD NOT be re-litigated by a future
  session unless a defined condition for reopening applies (§9).
- Conflict resolution MUST NOT leak the existence or contents of a
  restricted artifact to an unauthorized identity (§11).

## Normative Requirements

### §1 Conflict Definition

**DESIGN DECISION REQUIRED**

**Question:** What precisely counts as a conflict under MAK-0004, and
how is it distinguished from an apparent conflict caused by differing
scope or environment?

**Why it matters:** Over-broad conflict detection creates constant,
unnecessary escalation. Under-broad detection silently ignores real
contradictions.

**Possible approaches:**
- A: Conflict defined purely at the content level (two statements are
  logically incompatible), evaluated independently of scope.
- B: Conflict defined only after scope-matching — two statements are
  only "in conflict" if they apply to the same scope, environment, and
  context.
- C: A layered definition — content-level contradiction is flagged
  first, then scope/environment matching is applied as a filter to
  determine whether it's a genuine conflict.

**Trade-offs:** Option A is simpler to specify but will generate false
positives (see example below). Option B requires scope-matching logic
to exist and be reliable before conflict detection can run at all.
Option C is closer to what the examples in this document assume, but is
the most complex to specify precisely.

**Decision:** TBD
**Rationale:** TBD

**Illustrative example (non-normative):**
```
Artifact A: "New APIs should use Platform A."
Artifact B: "New APIs should use Platform B."
→ Likely a genuine conflict — same scope implied.

Artifact A: "Development environment uses Platform A."
Artifact B: "Production environment uses Platform B."
→ Likely NOT a conflict — different environments/scopes.
```

Categories to distinguish (content still TBD, not finalized):
- Direct contradiction
- Mutually exclusive instructions
- Incompatible constraints
- Apparent conflict caused by differing scope
- Apparent conflict caused by differing environment
- Historical information vs. current information (see §4, §5)

### §2 Authority

**DESIGN DECISION REQUIRED**

**Question:** Should MAK4I define a fixed authority hierarchy, allow
configurable authority classes, or some combination?

**Why it matters:** Not all artifact sources should carry equal weight
when they disagree. Without a defined answer, implementations will
diverge, and resolution outcomes become unpredictable across systems.

**Possible approaches:**
- A: A fixed authority hierarchy defined by this standard (e.g.,
  organization policy > team standard > project decision > user
  preference > AI-generated inference), applied uniformly.
- B: Configurable authority classes, where an organization defines its
  own hierarchy and MAK4I enforces whatever hierarchy is configured.
- C: A hybrid — MAK4I defines a small set of default tiers, which
  organizations MAY override or extend.

**Trade-offs:** A fixed hierarchy (A) is simplest to implement and
reason about but may not fit every organization's actual governance
model. Configurable hierarchies (B) are more flexible but harder to
certify consistently across implementations, and push complexity onto
every adopter. A hybrid (C) tries to balance both but is the largest
surface area to specify.

Potential authority sources to classify (not yet ranked):
- Organization policy
- Approved architecture decision
- Team standard
- Project decision
- Project context
- User preference
- Session summary
- AI-generated inference

These sources MUST NOT be assumed to carry equal authority by default.

**Rationale:** TBD

### §3 Scope

A more specific artifact MAY override a broader default artifact when
their scopes differ and the more specific scope is properly nested
within the broader one.

A more specific artifact MUST NOT automatically override a broader
artifact that carries organization-level authority over a matter such
as security, unless explicitly permitted by an authority rule defined
per §2.

**DESIGN DECISION REQUIRED**

**Question:** What is the canonical set of scope levels MAK4I
recognizes, and how is nesting/precedence between them defined?

**Why it matters:** Without an agreed scope taxonomy, "more specific"
is undefined, and the override rule above cannot be implemented
consistently.

**Possible approaches:**
- A: A fixed, ordered scope hierarchy (e.g., organization → business
  unit → team → project → environment → user → task → session).
- B: An unordered set of scope dimensions that must be explicitly
  compared per-conflict rather than assumed to nest linearly.

**Trade-offs:** A fixed linear hierarchy (A) is easy to reason about
but may not reflect real organizational structures, where "team" and
"environment" aren't naturally nested inside one another. An unordered
model (B) is more accurate to real-world structure but requires each
implementation to define comparison logic, weakening interoperability.

**Decision:** TBD
**Rationale:** TBD

*Illustrative scope levels under discussion (non-normative, not final):
organization, business unit, team, project, environment, user, task,
session.*

### §4 Version and Status

Conflict resolution MUST consider artifact lifecycle information —
including states such as active, deprecated, superseded, and
historical — when determining which of two applicable artifacts should
prevail.

Detailed lifecycle state definitions, transitions, and versioning
mechanics are defined in [MAK-0005](MAK-0005-template.md), not here. MAK-0004
MUST consume that information as an input to resolution rather than
redefine lifecycle semantics.

*Note: [MAK-0001](MAK-0001.md) currently defines only `deprecated` and
`deprecated_by` as lifecycle-adjacent fields. Whether MAK-0005 extends
these or introduces additional states (draft, active, superseded) is a
MAK-0005 decision this standard depends on — see MAK-0005 §4.*

### §5 Time and Recency

Newer information MUST NOT automatically prevail over older information
solely because it has a more recent timestamp.

**DESIGN DECISION REQUIRED**

**Question:** If recency is not the universal resolution mechanism, how
does time participate in resolution at all, and in what circumstances
(if any) does a newer artifact appropriately take precedence?

**Why it matters:** Without a defined role for time, the specification
risks either ignoring genuinely relevant recency signals, or leaving
implementations to reintroduce "newest wins" informally, defeating the
purpose of this requirement.

**Possible approaches:**
- A: Time is only a tiebreaker of last resort, used only when authority
  and scope are equal and no other rule applies.
- B: Time participates via explicit supersession only (see MAK-0005
  §8) — an artifact only "wins" over an older one if it explicitly
  supersedes it, never by timestamp comparison alone.
- C: Certain artifact types or authority tiers may define their own
  recency rules, configurable per-organization.

**Trade-offs:** Option A keeps time as a narrow fallback but still
requires defining what "equal" authority/scope means. Option B is the
cleanest and most consistent with §9's decision-continuity goal, but
removes time as a resolution signal entirely outside of supersession.
Option C reintroduces the variability problem §2 already raises for
authority.

**Decision:** TBD
**Rationale:** TBD

**Illustrative example (non-normative):**
```
Organization Security Policy — Created: January — Status: Active
Developer Note — Created: August — Status: Informational

The August note should NOT automatically override the January policy
merely because it is newer.
```

### §6 Explicit vs. Inferred Information

An explicitly approved decision MUST NOT be automatically overridden or
reinterpreted based on AI-generated summaries, inferred knowledge,
observations, or session notes that were not themselves explicitly
approved through the same or a comparable authority path.

**Illustrative example (non-normative):**
```
Approved architecture decision: "Database = PostgreSQL"
AI-generated session summary: "Developer considered MongoDB."

The summary MUST NOT be treated as converting consideration into a
superseding architecture decision.
```

**DESIGN DECISION REQUIRED**

**Question:** How does a resolution process reliably distinguish
"explicit/approved" from "inferred/observational" at the artifact
level — is this a required metadata field, an artifact-type distinction,
or something else?

**Why it matters:** Without a reliable signal for this distinction, §6
cannot be enforced mechanically — it would depend on the resolution
process correctly guessing provenance, which contradicts the intent of
this section.

**Possible approaches:**
- A: A required or well-known optional metadata field (e.g., an
  `approval_status` or similar) that a resolution process checks.
- B: Inferred purely from artifact `type` (per MAK-0001) — e.g.,
  episodic artifacts are treated as observational unless otherwise
  marked.
- C: Left undefined at the MAK-0004 level and deferred to MAK-0001 or a
  future metadata standard.

**Decision:** TBD
**Rationale:** TBD

### §7 Automatic Resolution

A MAK4I-compatible system MAY resolve a conflict automatically only
under conditions defined by this standard. Candidate conditions under
discussion (none finalized) include:

- One artifact explicitly supersedes another (per MAK-0005).
- One artifact is active and the conflicting artifact is superseded or
  deprecated (per MAK-0005 lifecycle state).
- Artifacts apply to different scopes and are therefore determined not
  to be in genuine conflict (per §1, §3).
- An explicit, approved decision overrides an inferred or observational
  artifact (per §6).

These candidate conditions are illustrative starting points only and
MUST NOT be treated as final normative rules unless confirmed by the
specification owners or supported by existing MAK4I documentation.

---

### §8 Escalation

When applicable artifacts are of equal authority and equal or
overlapping scope, and neither supersedes the other, and no automatic
resolution condition from §7 applies, a MAK4I-compatible system MUST
escalate the conflict rather than resolve it automatically.

The specific mechanism of escalation (e.g., returned as an unresolved
result, surfaced to a human reviewer, logged for later resolution) is
**TBD** and MUST be defined by the specification owners before this
section can be considered normative.

**Illustrative example (non-normative):**
```
Two active organization policies, same scope, same authority tier,
neither supersedes the other, direct contradiction.
→ Expected behavior likely involves exposing/escalating the conflict
  rather than silently selecting one — exact mechanism TBD.
```

---

### §9 Decision Continuity

If a valid, active decision has already resolved a given question, a
future AI session interacting with MAK4I-managed artifacts SHOULD NOT
re-treat that question as unanswered, unless one of the following
applies:

- The decision has become stale (staleness criteria: TBD, likely
  defined in coordination with MAK-0005).
- The decision was superseded (per MAK-0005).
- Conflicting authoritative information exists (per §2, §8).
- The decision was explicitly reopened.
- Required context for the decision is incomplete.

**Illustrative example (non-normative):**
```
Organization decision: "New API onboarding uses Platform B."
Rationale: "Platform A is being retired."
Status: Active.

A future AI session handling new API intake SHOULD begin from the
Platform B onboarding path rather than asking "Should we use Platform
A or Platform B?"
```

MAK-0004 defines the conflict-resolution aspects of this behavior only;
the underlying staleness and supersession mechanics belong to MAK-0005.

### §10 Resolution Record

**DESIGN DECISION REQUIRED**

**Question:** What must be recorded when a conflict is resolved, and
where/how is that record stored?

**Why it matters:** Without a defined resolution record, §9's
decision-continuity goal cannot function reliably — there would be no
durable trace of what was decided, why, or by what rule.

**Possible approaches:**
- A: A minimal record (conflicting artifact IDs, selected outcome,
  timestamp) stored as a new artifact type or extension.
- B: A fuller record including rejected/superseded artifact IDs,
  resolution rule applied, automatic-vs-human-approved flag, rationale,
  and the identity/authority responsible for manual resolution.
- C: Resolution records are out of scope for MAK-0004 and left to a
  future standard or implementation detail.

**Trade-offs:** Option A is lightweight but may not carry enough
information to prevent re-litigation (§9) or support an audit trail.
Option B directly supports §9 and traceability but adds real
specification and implementation surface. Option C simplifies MAK-0004
but may leave decision continuity unenforceable in practice.

**Decision:** TBD
**Rationale:** TBD

Candidate fields under discussion (not finalized): conflicting artifact
IDs, versions, selected artifact/outcome, rejected/superseded artifact,
resolution rule applied, timestamp, automatic-vs-human-approved flag,
rationale, identity/authority responsible for manual resolution.

### §11 Security

Conflict resolution MUST NOT expose the contents of a restricted
artifact to an identity that is not authorized to access it.

**DESIGN DECISION REQUIRED**

**Question:** Does even revealing that a restricted, conflicting
artifact *exists* (without exposing its content) constitute a
disclosure risk that must also be prevented?

**Why it matters:** In some cases, the mere existence of a conflicting
restricted artifact (e.g., "a conflicting security policy exists but
you cannot view it") could itself leak sensitive information depending
on context. This has real implications for how escalation (§8) is
implemented for an unauthorized identity.

**Possible approaches:**
- A: Existence may be disclosed, but content may not — an
  unauthorized identity sees "an unresolved conflict exists" without
  detail.
- B: Existence itself must be withheld from unauthorized identities —
  the conflict is silently omitted from what that identity sees.

**Trade-offs:** Option A is more transparent and easier to debug but
risks leaking sensitive signal through existence alone in some threat
models. Option B is more conservative but could make debugging or
escalation confusing for an unauthorized identity who sees no conflict
at all when one genuinely exists for authorized parties.

**Rationale:** TBD

## Interoperability Considerations

Because MAK4I is required to remain model-independent, tool-independent,
storage-independent, and provider-independent, this standard MUST
define conflict-resolution *behavior* rather than a specific
implementation, storage mechanism, or algorithm library. Any
authority-hierarchy or scope-taxonomy decision made in §2/§3 MUST be
expressible without assuming a particular registry, database, or SDK.

## Relationship to Other MAK Standards

- **MAK-0001** (Artifact Metadata Schema) — MAK-0004 consumes `id`,
  `version`, `type`, `deprecated`, and `deprecated_by` as defined
  there.
- **MAK-0002** (Context Injection Specification) — context selection
  and injection happen downstream of conflict resolution; MAK-0004
  does not define injection format.
- **MAK-0003** (Memory Resolution Algorithm) — relevance/applicability
  determination is expected to be a MAK-0003 concern; MAK-0004 assumes
  a set of "applicable artifacts" has already been determined.
- **MAK-0005** (Versioning and Compatibility) — MAK-0004 consumes
  lifecycle state and supersession information from MAK-0005 (see §4,
  §7, §9) rather than redefining it.
